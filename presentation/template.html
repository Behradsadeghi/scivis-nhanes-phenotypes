Limitswhat the answers to Q1–Q5 do and do not establish

What this establishes — and what it does not

Established

  • Four stable regions in the biomarker space (bootstrap ARI 0.89), each persisting at k ± 1.
  • They differ on 21 of 22 variables they never saw, with large effects on diagnosis, BMI, sex and age.
  • 31% of lab-defined diabetes is undiagnosed (31% weighted), concentrated outside the Dysglycaemic cluster.
  • A consistent signature: held-out recovery 0.98 against a 0.25 baseline.

Not established

  • That the phenotypes are natural groups: the data is one continuum (one density mode, silhouette 0.11).
  • Anything over time: NHANES is cross-sectional, with no follow-up outcome in our data.
  • Causation: income, sex and BMI are associations, and several act through each other.
  • US prevalence of the clusters: clustering is unweighted; diagnoses are self-reported.

Appendix

The seven questions every cluster analysis must answer, answered

1Which variables built the clusters, which describe them?14 biomarkers built them; 22 external variables describe them. No overlap.
2How many clusters, chosen how, and what happens at k ± 1?k = 4 by bootstrap stability (ARI 0.89 vs 0.77 at k = 5). k = 3 merges Inflammatory and Hepatic; k = 5 splits an older renal group.
3How many tests, which correction for which family?22 omnibus tests, Benjamini–Hochberg across variables; pairwise Mann–Whitney / Fisher with Holm within each variable.
4An effect size beside every p-value?Kruskal–Wallis ε², Cramér’s V, standardised differences, odds ratios per SD.
5Cluster sizes on every figure?810 · 581 · 660 · 239.
6Can a model recover the labels on held-out patients?Balanced accuracy 0.98 (L1 logistic), 0.92 (random forest); baseline 0.25.
7What do the clusters predict that the clustering never saw?Diagnoses, blood pressure, self-rated health, sex and income: external-only model 0.53 vs 0.25.
